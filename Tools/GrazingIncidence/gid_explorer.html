  Two ideas the panels are built around. First, the X-ray is a plane wave
  across a whole domain, so the amplitude is
  Σ exp(−i Q·r) — the Fourier transform of
  the density. Shifting a domain sideways multiplies it by
  exp(−i Q·Δ), which cancels in |F|²: only
  G matters, never where the domain sits. Second, a single domain meets
  Qxy = G at isolated azimuths only, but a monolayer is a mosaic
  covering every in-plane angle, so each |G| always has some domain in
  condition. The tilt azimuth rotates with its own domain, so
  Qz survives the averaging untouched.
   Fixed behind the scenes: αi = 0.11° (below
  αc ≈ 0.12° for water at 10 keV; it sets penetration
  depth, not the diffraction condition), ΔE/E = 1.4×10−4
  for Si(111), domain-size spread 20%.
   Not modelled: refraction and the Vineyard factor, polarisation and
  effective-area corrections, and the molecular form factor — so the real
  (02):(11) intensity ratio, which is sensitive to backbone-plane orientation, is
  not reproduced. Positions are exact. Peak heights and
  qz-integrated intensities are different quantities here: a
  rod centred at qz=0 is halved by the horizon, so the integrated
  (11):(02) ratio comes out near 2:1 while the heights are nearer 1:1.
   Detector axes. qz depends on αf alone,
  so the right-hand scale is exact; |Qxy| depends on
  αf as well as 2θ, so the top scale is drawn as the feet
  of its own iso-qxy curves and is read off the curve, not the edge.
  Indices names the peaks the image actually contains; ones that
  coincide exactly — (11) and (1̄1) under NN tilt — share a
  label, and ↓ marks a rod whose maximum is below the horizon.
